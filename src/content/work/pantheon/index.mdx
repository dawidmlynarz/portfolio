## Foundations first

I started with the foundations, because every component later leans on them. Typography pairs Poppins for headings with Aleo italic for editorial moments. Marketing pages get their own scale: 1.25 on desktop and a tighter 1.2 on mobile, so headings still fit on a small screen.

## Documentation people can use

A component is only half done until someone else can pick it up without asking me. Each page explains when to use a pattern and when not to, then shows every variant. Links and buttons were a good example: the button pyramid makes the hierarchy of primary, secondary and tertiary actions obvious at a glance.

<Figure
  src={linksButtons}
  alt="Documentation pages for links and buttons: usage guidelines, link styles in default and hover states, and a pyramid showing high, medium and low emphasis buttons."
/>

## Spacing and layout

Spacing follows a formula instead of a list of numbers someone has to remember. It uses a 1.25 Major Third scale, where 1rem is 16px on the smallest viewport and 24px on the largest. The grid goes from 4 columns on mobile to 6 on tablets and 12 on desktop, with examples of nested grids and fixed sidebars so engineers could see how it works in real product screens.
