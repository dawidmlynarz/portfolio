# Strawman workshop

## Ask for

- Discovery notes and anything the client has shared
- The workshop goal and who's attending
- Session length

## Step 1: Strawman

Draft the current state, opportunities and a first roadmap. Make every claim specific enough to be wrong, and mark each one: (from notes), (assumption) or (guess).

## Step 2: Readiness check

Before the session, check that every section names real systems or teams, that no section is only guesses, and that the five questions most likely to change the roadmap are listed.

## Step 3: Session plan

For the project manager: an agenda by section with timings, the question to ask at each section ("What did we get wrong here?"), and what to do if the client rejects the whole strawman (ask what the real picture is, and log it as one big correction).

## Step 4: Correction log

A table with: section, what we said, what they said, who, impact on scope, follow-up.

## Step 5: Proposal outline

After the session, turn the log into a proposal outline: scope from the "changes scope" rows, risks from the blockers, and the confirmed roadmap.

## Done when

- The project manager can run the session from the plan alone
- Every correction shows up in the proposal or the follow-ups
